I am a postdoctoral research fellow at the Computing and Software Department at McMaster University, with a background in Natural Language Processing (NLP) focusing on human argumentation and the explainability of machine learning models. My research explores the intersection of argumentation and explainability, driven by the increasing need to understand the behavior of AI systems.
I earned my **PhD in Computer Science from Paderborn University** (July 2018 - December 2023). My doctoral research resulted in the dissertation titled <a href='https://digital.ub.uni-paderborn.de/hs/content/titleinfo/7498576'>“Audience-Aware Argument Generation,”</a> aimed to advance the effectiveness of argument generation by emphasizing the importance of relevance, consideration of the opponent's argument, and addressing the audience's interests. As a **postdoc at Columbia University (January 2024 - June 2026)**, I developed methods for authorship attribution, with a particular emphasis on making these models explainable. This work involves interpreting latent spaces to understand what aspects of style authorship attribution models capture. Most recently, at **McMaster University, as  Postdoctoral Research Fellow**, I am investigating the mechanisms by which prompting AI to emulate a specific role can affect their subjective judgment.
